Designing for delight without the noise

2026-06-1206:24 read#design #craft
[ hero illustration · 1200×600 ]

Every product wants to feel “delightful” now. Confetti on checkout. A bouncing mascot. Three different toast animations fighting for attention. Somewhere along the way we confused delight with noise.

The noise problem

Noise is any motion, color, or sound that asks for attention it didn't earn. It's the interface equivalent of someone talking loudly on a quiet train. The first one is charming. The tenth is exhausting.

Delight is what's left after you remove everything that's just showing off.

Restraint as craft

The interfaces I keep coming back to share a trait: they're quiet by default and expressive on purpose. Motion exists to explain a change, not to decorate it.

// the rule I design by
if (motion.explains_a_change) keep();
else cut();

A small checklist

Before shipping any animation I ask: does it tell the user where something came from, where it went, or that it worked? If the answer is none of those, it's noise — and it goes.

Playful and grown-up aren't opposites. The trick is spending your delight budget on a few moments that matter instead of sprinkling it everywhere.
